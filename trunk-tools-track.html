01

Track Overview

What this track is, who it's for, and how "trained, used in the workflow, and assessed" gets applied to this specific tool.

Learning objective

A reviewer can independently evaluate a TrunkSubmittal-flagged discrepancy against the cited spec section, confirm or overturn the flag, and either resolve it or escalate it correctly.

What the agent actually does

When a submittal is received, TrunkSubmittal analyzes it against the project's specifications and relevant RFI responses, flags non-compliant line items, and generates a structured recommendation. Everything in this track teaches someone to work with that output.

Certification standard

Trained

Modules 1–3 complete — understands what the tool checks and can classify flag types correctly

Used in workflow

Module 4 — reviewed real flags on their own project's live submittal log

Assessed

Module 5 — correctly resolves or escalates flagged discrepancies independently

Sample Assessment

Two pieces of "assessments that make certification mean something": a knowledge-check question from Module 3, and the rubric an evaluator uses to certify Module 5.

Sample knowledge check — Module 03
Question 1 of 3 (sample)
TrunkSubmittal flags a waterproofing membrane submittal as partially compliant: the product model number matches spec, but the listed thickness is below the minimum called out in Section 07 13 00. What's the correct next step?
Approve the submittal, since the model number is the detail subcontractors get wrong most often.
Reject the submittal outright and ask the subcontractor to resubmit the full package.
Confirm the thickness gap against Section 07 13 00, then request updated product data for that item.Correct
Escalate to the architect directly, since any thickness deviation requires design review.
Sample knowledge check — Module 03
Question 2 of 3 (sample)
A flag cites an RFI response that appears to conflict with the spec section it's tied to. What should you do?
Trust the flag as written, since the system already checked both documents.
Follow the spec section only, since it's the controlling contract document.
Send the conflict back to the subcontractor and let them propose which document governs.
Read both documents yourself, since an RFI response can amend a spec and the two need reconciling.Correct

Module 5 — Certification rubric (live evaluator checklist)

CriterionPass
Correctly identifies whether each flag is compliant, partially compliant, or non-compliant✓
Cites the specific spec section (not just "the spec") when confirming or overturning a flag✓
Correctly overturns at least one deliberately-incorrect flag included in the assessment set, rather than approving it by default✓
Drafts a response to the trade partner that names the specific discrepancy, not a generic rejection✓
Completes all 5 flagged items within the session — no more than 1 requires evaluator prompting✓

The deliberately-incorrect flag (row 3) is the design decision that makes this assessment mean something — a reviewer who approves every flag without scrutiny fails, because the standard is judgment, not compliance with the tool's output.
